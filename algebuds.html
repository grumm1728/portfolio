Algebuds

Teachable agents have rich pedagogy history--how do LLMs support them?

Open prototype

inside-out tutoring

Algebuds is an experiment in turning the AI tutor relationship inside out. Instead of asking a chatbot for help, students become the teacher for a small eager robot learning Algebra 1. The robot pets ask questions, attempt problems, make mistakes, and ask for feedback. The student's job is not simply to get answers, but to help another learner develop understanding.

why robots lead

The idea grew from a frustration with a common chatbot learning pattern: students often do not know what questions to ask. A blank prompt box assumes a level of metacognition and confidence that many learners simply do not have yet. Students "don't know what they don't know." In Algebuds, the robots drive the interaction forward by surfacing confusion at the right level and inviting explanation, critique, and correction.

recursive feedback

Recursive feedback is the idea that tutors learn by watching their students use what they have been taught. In research by Okita and Schwartz, students who taught either human pupils or teachable agents and then observed those pupils attempt problems showed stronger transfer and problem-solving performance than students who simply received direct feedback themselves.

prototype loop

Algebuds is designed around this loop. The important moment is not merely explaining an algebra procedure, but seeing a robot pet try to use that explanation, noticing where its understanding breaks down, and then refining the teaching. The robot's mistakes become a visible mirror of the student's own partial understanding. By externalizing reasoning into worked algebra steps, misconceptions, corrections, and retries, the system aims to create a more active and reflective form of AI-supported learning.
